description_status: "This footage shows religious activities among prisoners of war filmed by Helms of the 167th Signal Company, 71st Signal Battalion, GHQ. The film was planned by the Civil Information and Education Section (CI&E) during the Korean War. On February 27, 1952, Brigadier General Robert A. McClure of the Department of the Army Office of the Chief of Psychological Warfare recommended that recreation and athletic competitions for POWs be included in the United Nations Command CI&E program for psychological warfare purposes (RG 319, Entry A1 154, Box 7, Assignment of Responsibility within Department of the Army for POW Reorientation Programs, 29 FEB 1952). While the Geneva Conventions explicitly provide for basic exercise for POWs, there is no provision for using such activities for reeducation or psychological warfare. Religious activities at the POW camp were conducted under the leadership of Harold Voelkel (1898-1975, Korean name Ok Ho-yeol), a missionary born in California and affiliated with the Northern Presbyterian Church. Voelkel and his wife began missionary work in Andong and other locations in Korea in August 1929. Following the forced withdrawal of missionaries under Japanese rule, he returned to Korea in 1946 to participate in the reopening of the Andong Mission and church reconstruction. In September 1950, he was commissioned as a U.S. Army chaplain and took part in the Incheon landing operation before immediately engaging in POW ministry. After the January 4, 1951 retreat, he served as a military chaplain at POW camps in Busan and Geoje. Voelkel coordinated POW camp ministry through the U.S. forces Chaplain Corps and the Northern Presbyterian Church overseas mission board. By June 1951, one United Nations Command Presbyterian chaplain, one Catholic chaplain, and four refugee Presbyterian pastors were active at the Geoje POW camp under the guise of POW ministry. See Lee Jong-man, Activities of the Northern Presbyterian Church Korean Mission Board during the Korean War - Focusing on Missionary Harold Voelkel, Ewha Historical Studies 40, 2010."
