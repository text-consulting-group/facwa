title: "Everyday Life at the Busan Prisoner-of-War Camp"
description_status: "This footage shows the everyday lives of prisoners at the Busan prisoner-of-war camp on February 16, 1951. Before the Civil Information and Education section was organized, prisoners in the camp were guaranteed the right to exercise. This was stipulated in the Third Geneva Convention, and the film shows major events such as calisthenics and boxing. It also shows boy-soldier prisoners and wounded prisoners being transported to Busan by ambulance. A separate hospital compound had been prepared in Busan. At this time, some prisoners were also being moved to the Geoje prisoner-of-war camp."
description: "Shot list"
shotlist: "# Roll 1 (0:10), slate: Prisoners are gathered and doing calisthenics. Military police watch them. The camera first shows a prisoner's shoes, then moves upward from the shoes to the upper body. The calisthenics continue. (1:30) The camera closes in on prisoners' faces. Most of them wear stern expressions. A burned prisoner appears, and the camera moves into a close-up of a prisoner's eyes. Prisoners without caps continue the exercises. A U.S. military policeman watches over the men doing calisthenics. 'PW' is written on a prisoner's trousers. The prisoners continue the exercises while shouting the cadence, 'one, two, three.' (4:03) The prisoners go into a building. Their height and weight are measured.\n\n# Roll 2 (4:25), slate: A sign appears reading, 'Attention! You will be here only temporarily. Cooperate with your leaders. Let each of us keep ourselves and our surroundings clean. (Processing Section).' Prisoners are weighed one by one. (4:59) Two prisoners wearing boxing gloves are fighting. The match is intense from the beginning. The two men are of completely different builds, and the taller boxer appears to have the advantage. Other prisoners cheer for them. The prisoners seem to be enjoying the event. Another pair of boxers then appears and the boxing continues. This pair has a similar build. (6:52) Female prisoners and male prisoners climb down from a truck. All are prisoners of the North Korean People's Army. They are all dressed in winter clothing, and many boy-soldier prisoners are mixed among them. They gather beside an ambulance. Some prisoners are wounded. The U.S. soldiers load only the wounded prisoners into the ambulance. (8:40) A wounded prisoner is shown in close-up."
