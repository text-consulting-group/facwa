description_status: "This footage shows a sports festival scene at Geoje Prisoner of War Camp, composed of multiple events. The large-scale camp sports festival on February 11, 1952 included not only track and field events but also boxing and various other competitions. In the footage, scenes of runners bending at the waist, long-distance running, and three boxing matches are shown. This was a physical activity program conducted by the Civil Information and Education (CIE) Division. The CIE education department referred to physical activities as "recreational games," organized under a recreational games officer with unit education leaders and barracks leaders, mobilizing sports, art, theater, music, painting, and other activities. Each unit commander was required to maintain a sports ground within their battalion and equip it with shared facilities such as basketball and volleyball courts, wrestling areas, and boxing rings. The activities were not limited to simple sports; various anti-communist slogans and songs were introduced. Drawing on experience from reeducating prisoners of war since World War II, the U.S. military established interrogation, indoctrination, and training centers, planning reeducation in the form of "brainwashing" not only for North Korean People's Army but also Chinese People's Volunteers during the Korean War. The educational objectives were: (1) to enhance prisoners of war's understanding and sympathy for UN political, social, and economic goals and activities; (2) to enable prisoners of war to understand democratic processes through camp activities; (3) to promote vocational and technical skills for prisoners of war to participate in future national reconstruction while improving camp life; (4) to develop the knowledge, understanding, and attitudes needed for North Korean prisoners of war to participate in building an independent and unified Korea; and (5) to train selected prisoners of war as key personnel for reeducating North Koreans about UN goals and activities. Following these objectives, the first reeducation experiment was conducted with 500 prisoners of war from October to December 1950, and based on the results, the CI&E under the GHQ took charge from June 1951. The CI&E was the department responsible for reeducating Japanese after the war, and from April 3, 1951, it was reorganized under the United Nations Command headquarters by General Order No. 8, expanding its internal organization (Executive Office, Field Operations, Educational Materials Publishing) and appointing Korean civilian personnel. Reeducation included ideological reeducation as indoctrination-style "brainwashing," as well as religion, sports, vocational education, radio education, and various other forms. Winners carrying flags are received favorably by officers and walk toward a tower-shaped building. At the tower-shaped building, they receive trophies or awards and salute in gratitude.
