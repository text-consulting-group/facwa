title: "Munsan-ri Wounded Prisoner Exchange Operation (Little Switch)"
description_status: "This footage shows interview content about the roles of Mobile Hospital No. 45 and the Munsanli Railway Transport Headquarters during the wounded prisoner of war exchange (Little Switch) operation on April 15, 1953. Wounded prisoners of war were transported from Geoje or Busan hospital camps to Munsan Station. The wounded prisoner of war operation proceeded as follows: Wounded prisoners from Geoje Camp were transported to Busan Port, stayed at Busan Hospital Camp, then were transported by special wounded prisoner of war train via Daegu-Yeongdeungpo-Munsan Station. Returning prisoners stayed briefly at the Yeongdeungpo Temporary Camp in Munsan. ROK forces prisoners were sent to the Yeongdeungpo Army Hospital, while U.S. forces prisoners were transported to the Army Hospital in Tokyo, Japan for treatment. ROK returning prisoners were processed with other prisoners of war at the Yongchodo Return Assembly Point from August 1953 onward and received reeducation. [NEEDS REVIEW]"

Hollingsworth), 45th Army Mobile Surgical Hospital adjutant, explains the wounded prisoner of war exchange process and the handling of returning wounded prisoner of war on April 15, 1953. In an interview outside the hospital tent, he stated his unit could handle necessary emergency surgical treatment for returning prisoner of war. He also explained the hospital establishment and that conducting trial operations on patients could expedite the repatriation process. (3min45sec) Brigadier General Ginn L. Holmes Jr. (1902-1971), U.S. 8th Army Medical Corps commander, continues the interview. Ginn explains this operation was the 8th Army medical team's wounded prisoner of war operation prepared to handle casualties. He outlines the procedure of sending wounded from the 45th Mobile Hospital to Yeongdeungpo temporary camp and then transporting them to Japan. He emphasized that transport speed depends on each prisoner of war health condition. # Take 1-11(5min46sec) Slate Interview at Munsanri Railway Transportation Office (R.T.O) building with two soldiers. Private First Class Hall (D. Hall) and Naylor (E. Naylor) explain how the base and facilities operate outdoors to support the Little Switch operation and repatriation/return of prisoner of war. They explain how services were extended to support prisoner of war in conducting the Little Switch operation in a devastated area. [NEEDS REVIEW]"
