description_status: "This footage shows the prisoner of war registration process and various newsreel films used for reeducation. The Korean War prisoner of war registration manual originated from the U.S. forces manuals used for German and Japanese prisoners during World War II. The U.S. Army Military Police initially used Field Manual 19-40 (FM 19-40, 'Handling Prisoners of War in the Field') at the start of the Korean War, then revised it based on the 1949 Geneva Convention effective May 1, 1952, and revised it again on November 3. FM 19-40 covered general provisions, interrogation, methods of capture, collection and movement, camp facilities, repatriation methods, and the duties of military police companies handling prisoners of war. These duties were carried out by prisoner of war processing companies and escort companies. General provisions included searching the bodies of prisoners immediately upon capture, confiscating their personal belongings and documents, and transporting them to prisoner of war collection points. Camps were divided into collection points, temporary camps, and main camps. Prisoners arriving at collection points were given POW tags bearing their number and name to wear around their necks. They then filled out individual prisoner of war registration cards with their name, rank, home address, residence, and unit. Prisoners transferred to main camps underwent delousing, received various supplies, and were further processed with fingerprinting and photography (front and side views) in addition to basic information. During the Korean War, over 180,000 prisoners were held, and registration was not completed for some prisoners even just before repatriation."
