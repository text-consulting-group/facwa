title: "(North Korean Captured Footage) North Korean Psychological Warfare Using ROK and U.S. Prisoners of War"
description_status: "This footage depicts ROK and U.S. forces prisoners of war being exploited for psychological warfare purposes on the streets of Seoul during the Korean War. It was filmed directly by a North Korean Korean People's Army communications unit soldier and was captured by U.S. forces after the recapture of Seoul in September 1950. Notably, the footage contains approximately 100 actual still photographs that were excavated and collected by a research team. There are no previously known public cases of ROK and U.S. POW footage in Korea. In mid-July 1950, ROK and U.S. prisoners captured in Seoul and other areas were temporarily held at the Government Building and various locations across the city before being used for psychological warfare. The captured ROK and U.S. prisoners were forcibly mobilized by North Korean forces and compelled to participate in anti-American war intervention protests, marches, and press conferences. The footage shows the streets of Seoul and its citizens under North Korean occupation. Of particular note, photographs of ROK and U.S. prisoners delivered by Life magazine to the U.S. Army in 1955 depict not only press conferences but also marches, the processing of ROK prisoners, and scenes of captured prisoners moving beneath the Han River Bridge."
