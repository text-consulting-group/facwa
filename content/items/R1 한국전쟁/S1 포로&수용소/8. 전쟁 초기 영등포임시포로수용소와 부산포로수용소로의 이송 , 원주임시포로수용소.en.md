title: "Early War: Transport to Yeongdeungpo Temporary POW Camp and Busan POW Camp / Wonju Temporary POW Camp"
description_status: "This footage shows the early war Yeongdeungpo Temporary Prisoner of War Camp and the assembly camp Wonju Temporary Prisoner of War Camp No. 2. The Yeongdeungpo Temporary Camp was established within a U.S. forces base facility in former Japanese military buildings, including scenes of wounded prisoners of war being transported, interiors of Mobile Hospital No. 121, and various other scenes. Notably, prisoners of war are shown being transported by train from Yeongdeungpo Temporary Camp to Busan Prisoner of War Camp No. 1 for processing. After disembarking from the train, prisoners of war are seen marching on foot to Busan Camp and receiving blankets and various supplies. Following this, the Wonju U.S. forces base camp where prisoners of war were assembled and processed was a type of assembly camp, smaller in scale than the Yeongdeungpo Temporary Camp. According to Red Cross records, Wonju was classified as the United Nations Command Temporary Prisoner of War Camp No. 2 and a transport prisoner of war camp. This camp operated until September 14, 1951. (\"ICRC CAMP REPORT – KOREA\", RG 59, Miscellaneous Records of the Bureau of Public Affairs, 1944–1964, Entry A1 1587-G, Box 7, 1952.7.23, NARA). Korean War prisoner of war camps were divided into assembly points, temporary camps, and designated camps. Temporary camps processed prisoners from assembly points for up to one month, conducting registration and interrogation before transporting them to Busan or Geoje. The distinctive feature of this footage is that it shows prisoner of war life at both the Yeongdeungpo and Wonju temporary camps, as well as the early appearance of the United Nations Command Busan Prisoner of War Camp No. 1. These camps held prisoners regardless of ideology or repatriation status, classified only by nationality, rank, and gender. [NEEDS REVIEW]"
