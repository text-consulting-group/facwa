description_status: "This footage shows Daeseongdong village, commonly known as 'Freedom Village,' a strategic hamlet. Following the repatriation agreement of February 3, 1954, Civil Control Line villages were established. During this period, 62 Self-Reliant Stability Villages (61 locations) and one Demilitarized Zone (DMZ) village were designated within the Civil Control Line areas of Ganghwa, Gimpo, Paju, and other locations within 25 kilometers of the Military Demarcation Line. Self-Reliant Stability Villages were areas with relatively relaxed military control; residents were primarily original inhabitants and relocated families including those with cross-border farming rights, and except for entry restrictions, the villages resembled ordinary farming communities. DMZ villages, unlike Self-Reliant Stability Villages, were created to counter North Korean propaganda villages. Daeseongdong 'Freedom Village' was both a DMZ village and a Strategic Hamlet, with extremely strict residency requirements: only those who had completed military service, were physically healthy and capable of farming, had sound ideology and no criminal record, and were free from alcoholism, theft, and debt could be admitted. Strategic Hamlets referred to rural villages created by U.S. forces in 1962 to separate residents of North Vietnam and South Vietnam. Daeseongdong village was designated as a DMZ village on August 3, 1953, and on March 11, 1960, U.S. forces conducted an inspection and evaluation of the village housing project. The housing consisted of 30 households with 27 pyeong units, and the village hall was 14 pyeong, smaller than the residential units. Construction involved the ROK forces Civil Affairs Division, U.S. forces Engineer units, and others. The first phase of housing construction began in October 1959, with school buildings and the village hall constructed under the U.S. forces Military Assistance Program. The footage shows Daeseongdong village housing, the village hall, and the activities of village residents."
